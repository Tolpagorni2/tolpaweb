Replatforming

An investigation into what happens to a business when the platform underneath it is replaced. Started September 2026, with three industrial partners.

the questiondeveloping knowledge
replatforming · developing knowledge

What should change, and what should be protected, when the platform underneath a business is replaced?

example investigation, partners and dates

backgroundwhy it matters

Most technology-intensive companies replace a core platform once or twice a decade. The project is usually planned as a technical migration, but it quietly changes how the company sells, prices, supports and learns.

The cases so far suggest that the most valuable things lost in a replatforming are rarely in the requirements. They are habits, shortcuts and small pieces of judgement that the old platform made possible without anyone deciding it should.

hypotheseswhat we think, for now
  • The parts of a business that move slowest are the ones most damaged by a fast replatforming.open
  • Companies that map their speed layers before choosing a platform make fewer late changes.supported
  • Replatforming is mainly a cost question.dropped
the workwhat we are doing

Interviews with leaders and the people closest to the platform in three companies, a review of project documents, and one workshop per case where the findings are tested with the people who lived them.

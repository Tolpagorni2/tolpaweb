Knowledge travels like a song

On why the knowledge that works best is the knowledge that can be carried, and what it loses on the way.

thought 0429 October 20263 min readsubjectsknowledgespeed layers

Knowledge is not an isolated object. It lives, changes, and takes new forms as it moves between people, teams, organisations and situations. The same song can be performed by different musicians, with different instruments, in different settings. It remains recognisable, but the sound changes.

That is why knowledge needs to be designed for travel. When knowledge becomes a model, it becomes easier to carry. It also becomes easier to detach from the situation where it was created. A framework that was born in one company's hard year arrives in another company's easy one, and it sounds different there.

A model is a song written down. Someone still has to play it.

In practice this means two things. The first is to send the context along with the model: what problem it answered, what it was tested against, and where it failed. The second is to expect the model to change in the hands of the people who use it, and to listen for those changes, because they are often where the next piece of knowledge is.

Some knowledge should not travel at all. It belongs to one place and one group of people, and it loses its meaning when it is lifted out. Part of the work is telling the two apart.

knowledgepage 1 of 4

One long scroll essay

The essay starts with the paradox: never has so much knowledge-like material been available, yet organisations still struggle to act on it. Then it shows the four places knowledge is made: academic research, consulting, inside organisations, and the media industry. As the reader scrolls, the northern lights appear at the moment the text says knowledge emerges from interaction. Situations create the environment, perspectives introduce variation, dialogue acts as the catalyst.

Then the reader reaches precepts and capabilities, and the page ends with "An Invitation" from the Foundation text.

The namea short story, told once

tolpagorni is not the summit. It is the peak you see all the way, the one that guides you there.

On the way to Kebnekaise, tolpagorni with its crater is always in view, and travellers use it to find the top. On an old map the Sami had named only that mountain: the useful one, the guide.

Your own photos, used small. The full story lives on the about page; I'll check the historical details before anything is published.

    Home, research with the status board, one group page and one magnus meets episode, in the real design.

  5. ongoing

    A living site

    Monthly status updates, an episode at a regular pace, field notes as groups climb.

Ideas for later versions

The lights follow activity

The aurora gets brighter when groups have been active recently, and dimmer in quiet months. The sky shows that the house is working.

The question of the week

One open question on the home page, over the moving lights, changing every week and linking to the group working on it.

Expeditions

Over the next year the knowledge work grows a lot. Industry friends who want to keep working on gnarly problems could join as expedition members: a named group per problem, travelling together with the guide.

Your route

Inside LearnWorlds, a member sees their own path: which groups they follow and how far up they have come.

The tenth perspective

From The Product Perspective: visitors can leave their own answer to an episode's question, and the best answers are published.
